ℹ️ Explanation of Evaluation Metrics

Precision and Recall

  • Precision: Of all items the system produced, what fraction were correct?
  • Recall: Of all items in the ground truth, how many did the system reproduce?

  • Structural precision/recall measure correctness and completeness of RDF triples (graph structure). Uses canonicalized IDs, so that systemic numbering misalignments due to different categorization are not scored wrongly (f.e.: T34 vs N1 and then , T35 vs T34)
  • Content fidelity precision/recall measure correctness and completeness of literal values, names, amounts, and other identifier strings.
  • Exact triple match precision/recall measure correctness and completeness of fully specified RDF statements, evaluated as strict string matches of subject–predicate–object triples.

Macro- and Micro-Average

  • Macro-average: Each entry counts equally, regardless of size (average of per-entry scores).
  • Micro-average: All items across all entries are pooled, giving larger entries more weight.

  • Macro-average example: If two entries score 1.0 and 0.0, macro-average F1 = (1.0 + 0.0) / 2 = 0.5.
  • Micro-average example: If one entry has 100 correct items and another has 0 out of 1, micro recall = 100 / 101 ≈ 0.99.

Canonicalized IDs for Structural Scoring

  • Canonicalized IDs are used for Triple based Scoring. That means that each entry ID (T1, T2, TT1, N1, etc.) has been standardized to "E" in the background so that systemic numbering misalignments due to different categorization are not scored wrongly. (f.e.: T34 vs N1 and then , T35 vs T34 but same entry and same encoding).
  • Transfers and Economic asset IDs are not canonicalized, cause they count up from 0 in each entry.
  • The comparison still shows and highlights the IDs as they are without canonicalization but does not score it based on the ID.
